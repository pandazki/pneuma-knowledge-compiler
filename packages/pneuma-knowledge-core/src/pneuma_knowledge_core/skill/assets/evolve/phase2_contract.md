The main verb is **moving, not rewriting**. That discipline governs the whole pass:

- **Prefer moving to writing.** When a claim belongs in a new family, move it along with its anchor comment, **verbatim** — do not change the wording, do not rephrase, do not "improve it while you are there". Anchor identity must stay continuous, so that the L3 projection, the event stream and git blame all keep following it. You should barely need to produce any newly written claim; this is not a recompile.
- **What does not fit stays where it is.** Move forward only meaning that clearly belongs to one of the new families. Anything you cannot place, anything of ambiguous shape, anything that could plausibly go into two families, stays in topics — "unclassified → accurately classified" is a one-way flow; you do not shuttle things between families, and you do not force a classification to make up numbers. Staying in topics is not a failure.
- **Merging redundancy is the exception, not the goal.** Merge two claims only when they really are semantically equivalent, and be able to say for every merge **why these two are equivalent** (same entity, same semantic scope, same certainty). A merge makes an anchor disappear, and the mechanism layer mechanically collects genuinely vanished anchors into a dropped-anchors list for review; you do not merge to "look tidier", and when unsure whether two are equivalent you do not merge — both stay.
- **The citation standard does not drop.** You can reach the old knowledge base and the raw material (`store.fetch` pulls source blocks on demand); every citation after a move, or newly written, is validated live against the store — the source must exist and the ¶ interval must fall inside its bounds. Moving verbatim preserves the original citation by construction; when a citation genuinely must change, check it to the same standard, and never write a citation that cannot reach its evidence just to save effort.
